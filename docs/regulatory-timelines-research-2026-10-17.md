## 1. BIR CAR + Registry of Deeds title transfer (land purchase)

**Official target:** BIR Citizen's Charter — eCAR (Certificate Authorizing Registration) issued in **5–15 working days** from formal acceptance of a complete application. [verified — Respicio & Co. commentary, "How Long Does BIR eCAR/CAR Processing Take?", lra.gov.ph FAQ; accessed 2026-10-17]

**Observed band:**
- Practitioner guides put BIR CAR itself at **2–4 weeks** normally, **15–30 days** depending on the RDO and case complexity. [band — federalland.ph key knowledge hub; keyrealty.ph; accessed 2026-10-17]
- The **full chain** (notarized deed → tax payments → eCAR → Registry of Deeds new title → new tax declaration at the Assessor) runs **~30–90 days** in a clean case; **3–6 months** when it goes sideways. [band — keyrealty.ph FAQ stage table (title verification 3–7 wd; eCAR 2–4 wks; RD transfer ~20 wd; assessor update 1–4 wks); Facebook practitioner posts converge on "3 weeks to 3 months, longer if complex"; accessed 2026-10-17]

**What actually causes the long delays (2025–2026 pattern):**
1. **Seller-side defects**: seller's unpaid old real-property taxes, estate/inheritance titles still in a decedent's name, unreleased old title, seller's own BIR registration problems. Buyer-side CAR for a *purchase* is one of the faster transactions; inherited-land transfers are the multi-month ones. [band]
2. **Missed tax deadlines**: CGT must be paid **within 30 days of notarization** of the Deed of Sale; DST by the **5th day of the following month** — miss them and you add 25% surcharge + 12%/yr interest and the CAR clock restarts at the RDO. This is the #1 self-inflicted delay for buyers who "close first, think taxes later." [verified — Respicio eCAR article]
3. **RDO workload / zonal-valuation verification requests** (examiner asks for more documents) — adds 2–6 weeks each cycle. [band]
4. **RD-side digitization**: Registry of Deeds converting manual titles; LRA FAQ notes manually issued titles need extra validation (eTitle claim 1 wd only for already-digitized titles). Isabela's RD is on the PHILARIS digitization track; a first-generation manual OCT converts slower. [band — lra.gov.ph FAQ; accessed 2026-10-17]

**The standard workaround — possession + CAR in parallel (does building/leasing start while transfer is pending?):**
- **Yes for construction and leasing.** LGU building-permit checklists across the country (Pasig 2024 checklist, General Trias, Naga OBO) explicitly accept a **notarized Deed of Absolute Sale + seller's title + authorization**, or a current tax declaration, as proof of rights over the land when the registered owner is not yet the applicant. LGUs in Cagayan Valley practice the same. So the buyer can file the building permit and build **in M1–M2** while the CAR/RD transfer runs to ~M3–M4 in the background. [verified (documented LGU checklists) + band (provincial practice)]
- **No for** secured bank financing against the new title, and no for titling-dependent steps (some OBOs/BPLOs ask for the updated title or new tax declaration at final CO/business-permit issuance — usually satisfied by DOAS + updated tax declaration in the buyer's name; confirm with Cauayan OBO per project). [band]
- Sequencing rule for all purchase plans: **pay CGT/DST inside the deadlines, file CAR immediately at M0+30d, don't wait for the new TCT to start design/permit/construction.** The AGY 90–180-day flag is right about the *titling* duration but wrong if it implies construction must wait — the realistic blocking case is only when the seller's side is dirty (estate issues, agricultural classification — see DAR risk in Notes).

## 2. BFP FSIC → Occupancy Permit → electric-meter energization

**Process chain (two BFP clearances, commonly confused):** [verified — AEDO Construction guides 2026 (FSIC guide dated 2026-08-24; Occupancy Permit guide 2026); RA 9514 Fire Code; PD 1096 §309; accessed 2026-10-17]
1. **FSEC** (Fire Safety Evaluation Clearance) — BFP reviews plans **before construction**; prerequisite to the building permit itself.
2. Build → Certificate of Completion (architect/engineer/owner) → as-built plans.
3. **FSIC** (Fire Safety Inspection Certificate) — BFP physically inspects the finished building against the FSEC-approved plans; prerequisite to the **Occupancy Permit**. RA 11032 (Ease of Doing Business Act) mandates BFP issue FSEC/FSIC **within 7 working days**. [verified — EODB Section 12 fire-safety timeline]
4. **Occupancy Permit / Certificate of Occupancy** — PD 1096 §309: Building Official must issue **within 30 days** after final inspection + complete submission. Cauayan's own published target: **5–10 wd after final inspection**. [verified]
5. Then CFEI (electrical final inspection) → **meter application** → energization.

**Realistic calendar time for a small commercial / 2-storey boarding build:** CO + FSIC together typically **6–12 weeks** *after construction ends* — not because of the OBO, but because (a) BFP inspection scheduling, (b) Notice-to-Comply corrections (signage, extinguisher class, egress) → re-inspection cycle, (c) as-built plans that must exactly match the FSEC. The single most common stall: fire details changed during construction that weren't in the approved FSEC. [band — AEDO 2026 guides; Respicio FSIC "Common Causes of Delay"; accessed 2026-10-17]

**Meter energization relative to CO (ISELCO-I / electric co-op):** utilities require proof of occupancy + CFEI before a permanent meter; ordinary service applications energize in **~2–4 weeks** once documents are in (Meralco model; co-ops are slower and ISELCO-I publishes no SLA — modeled at 2–6 weeks). The known "2–4 months blocking energization" pattern is really the **CO/FSIC phase itself** when fire paperwork is started only at project completion; once the CO is in hand, energization is weeks, not months. [band + modeled]

**Mitigation:** file FSEC during design (not at permit filing), request BFP pre-completion inspection at ~90% build, keep as-built = FSEC-approved plans, file the ISELCO-I application the day CFEI is issued.

## 3. Cauayan City LGU building-permit durations (2026)

Cauayan is one of the faster LGUs in the country on paper — it has an **e-BPLS electronic business-permit system** (elgu-city-of-cauayan-isabela.e.gov.ph) and was a **PCCI "Most Business-Friendly LGU" national finalist (Top 5) in 2024 and 2026**. [verified — cauayan city gov news; Luzon Morning Sun 2026-09; accessed 2026-10-17]

Official published Cauayan targets (cityofcauayan.gov.ph permits page; accessed 2026-10-17): [verified]
- **Building Permit: 5–15 working days** from filing of complete plans (varies by occupancy classification).
- **Occupancy Permit: 5–10 working days** after final inspection.
- **Locational/Zoning Clearance (CPDO): single-digit wd official; commonly 1–2 weeks realistic** incl. site inspection/revisions. [band] — prerequisite for both building and business permits.
- **New business permit (BPLO): same day to 3 wd** for complete applications (one-stop shop).

**What the published numbers miss (realistic 2026 total pre-build time):** the 5–15 wd assume a **complete permit package** — sealed architectural/structural/electrical/sanitary plans + FSEC. Realistic elapsed time from "decide to build" to "permit in hand" for these projects is **6–10 weeks**, dominated by design-professional drafting/revision time (4–8 wks) and any FSEC iteration, not by the LGU counter. [band — AEDO building-permit guide 2026 + practitioner consensus]

## 4. Typhoon climatology — Cagayan Valley / Isabela build windows

**Facts:** [verified — PAGASA TC information page; U.S. Embassy PH Weather Alert 2026-05-28; WWA/Carbon Brief 2024-season analysis; accessed 2026-10-17]
- ~20 tropical cyclones enter the PAR per year, ~8–9 cross the country; **~70% form July–October** (PAGASA), **85–90% occur May–October** (Embassy 2026), with a documented late-season danger band **late October–mid-November** (six storms struck PH in 30 days in Oct–Nov 2024).
- **Northern Luzon (Cagayan Valley/Isabela) is the most frequently hit region** — Cauayan sits in the standard track corridor. Recent consecutive seasons prove it: Egay (Jul 2023), Goring (Aug–Sep 2023), Enteng (Sep 2024), Kristine (Oct 2024), Pepito (Nov 2024), Nando (Sep 2025, Cat-5 landfall Cagayan), Paolo (Oct 2025, landfall Dinapigue, **Isabela**). Expect **several warning-level passes per season**, not one.

**Realistic build-stop per season (light CHB build):** [modeled — from the above + standard PH construction-practice commentary (PH constructors' dry-season guidance; UGC/rainy-season construction articles)]
- Each significant typhoon pass: 3–5 days of work stoppage (prep + storm) **plus** 2–5 days lost to post-storm cleanup, saturated ground, remobilization — i.e. ~1–2 weeks lost per direct pass. Isabela sees repeated passes + monsoon rain Jun–Nov, so **an open-earth/oopen-shell Jun–Nov build realistically loses 3–6 working weeks** cumulatively; a build that only finishes interior works during that season loses ~1–2 weeks.
- **Standard practice to recover the time:** foundations + CHB masonry + **roof closed by early–mid September**; schedule exterior works Dec–May (dry window); use the Sep–Dec months for covered interior fit-out, FSEC/FSIC/CO paperwork, meter applications, staffing — none of which stop for weather. Builders consistently rate **December–April as the prime build window** and warn against starting major works at wet-season peak.

## 5. PD 856 sanitary-code clearance + DOH permits for a funeral establishment

**The sequence for a Category II establishment (chapels, NO embalming on site — plan 8's model):** [verified — PD 856 (1975) Ch. XXI; DOH AO 2010-0033 IRR "Disposal of Dead Persons"; corroborated at LGU level by Naga City 2025 funeral-establishment ordinance; accessed 2026-10-17]
1. **DOH-HFSRB Initial Permit** — required **BEFORE construction** of any funeral establishment.
2. Build per PD 856 chapel sanitary requirements (potable water, gendered toilet/shower per chapel, parking/garage, waste bins, potable-water certification).
3. **DOH Operational Permit** from the regional CHD (Cagayan Valley CHD-II, Tuguegarao) — issued only after post-construction inspection and compliance check; this is the basis for the LGU sanitary permit.
4. **Sanitary Permit** from the Cauayan City Health Office — annual, valid to Dec, renewed yearly; IRR mandates the inspector conduct the sanitary inspection **within 7 days** of payment of the inspection fee. [verified — PD 856 IRR §10.1]
5. In parallel at OBO/BFP: locational clearance, building permit → completion → FSIC → Occupancy Permit → business permit.
6. **Category II operational requirement:** notarized **MOA with a Category I establishment within 50 km** to provide embalming services — a pre-operational contract dependency that must be secured, not assumed.

**Realistic durations:** DOH initial permit **3–8 weeks** (modeled — no SLA published; DOH Citizen's Charter governs but wasn't retrieved at national level for this tranche); post-construction CHD inspection → operational permit **3–6 weeks** (modeled); sanitary permit **1–2 weeks** (verified cadence above + band on fee payment lead time). No embalmer on staff needed for Category II.

**RA 9829 (Pre-Need Code) constraint:** applies **only if the operator sells pre-need plans directly as principal**. Securing a new pre-need operator license (trust/deposit requirements, capital, approvals) is a multi-year undertaking — not a Y1 milestone. If plan 8 operates on a pay-at-need/at-service model, RA 9829 is not a launch blocker; confirm the business model wording before treating it as one. [verified scope of the law + modeled on process difficulty]

## 6. FDA LTO for a bakery — 30–90 day claim

- **Claim verified in band:** FDA guidance/summaries state the FDA **aims to issue the LTO within 30–90 days** after receiving a complete application; practitioner guides (Emerhub) say **6–8 weeks**. So 30–90 days holds as the **official target band**, with ~6–10 weeks the realistic norm. [band — Scribd FDA LTO requirements/timeline doc; Emerhub guide updated 2025-08-11; accessed 2026-10-17]
- **What determines where you land in that band:** (1) **establishment type — a manufacturer/premises-based bakery undergoes an on-site pre-inspection before the initial LTO issues** (Triple I guide; FDA AO 2024-0015 procedures); inspection availability + any corrective actions add 2–6 weeks; (2) **application completeness/e-services pre-assessment bounce-backs**; (3) FDA processing backlogs.
- **2025–2026 rule changes worth knowing (initial application):** AO 2024-0015 (issued 2024-11-27, effective **2025-01-12**) restructured LTOs — initial validity **3 years for micro/small enterprises** (larger for medium/large), replacing the old regime. The 2025 renewal-window changes (180-day early renewal policy lifted 2025-07-08, back to 90 days) affect renewals, not initial issuance. [verified — FDA Advisory 2025-0095 & 0095-A; AO 2024-0015 references]
- **Bakery note:** if bread is sold fresh to walk-in customers only, LGU sanitary permit rules dominate; an FDA **LTO + per-product CPR** is required once you produce/pack/wholesale food products. Schedule the FDA inspection request to land when the kitchen is ~80–100% fitted out (inspection needs working premises).

## RA 9646 note (audit flag, applies to contractor engagement)
Construction contracting must be done through properly licensed contractors (PCAB license under RA 4566 / Contractors' License Law); design documents must be signed/sealed by licensed professionals. The AGY "RA 9646 exposure" flag concerns civil-engineering practice scope; for these plans the practical mitigation is: engage a PCAB-licensed contractor for the building phase and one coordinating engineer/architect for the permit package. [band — RA 4566 text; PCAB/DTI-CIAP guidance; accessed 2026-10-17]

## NOTES FOR USER

- **Titling is a background track, not a gate.** Pay the taxes inside the deadlines (CGT ≤30 days from notarization, DST by the 5th of the next month), then build on the strength of the notarized deed while BIR/RD process in parallel (2–3 months normal, up to 6 if the seller's side is dirty). The new TCT only truly blocks financing and resale — cash builds don't wait for it.
- **The real revenue wall is FSIC + Occupancy Permit + meter, and it's startable early.** The Cauayan CO itself is fast (5–10 wd official, 30-day statutory cap); what stalls projects 2–4 months is fire paperwork started only at completion. File the FSEC with the design, schedule the BFP inspection ~2 months before target completion, and keep the build identical to the approved FSEC plans. Electricity (ISELCO-I) follows within ~2–6 weeks after the CO/CFEI.
- **Typhoon season is a schedule input, not force majeure.** Isabela gets repeated passes Jun–Nov (peak Jul–Oct; late Oct–Nov 2024 saw a six-storm barrage). A 2-storey CHB build that keeps its roof off through Sep–Nov should budget 3–6 lost working weeks. Build Dec–May where possible and design the plan so Sep–Nov is interior-finishing and permit-chasing time, not open-shell time.
- **Plan 8's "PD 856 certification at M3" milestone is wrong but the launch window survives.** The correct chain is DOH Initial Permit (pre-construction) → build → DOH Operational Permit → city sanitary permit → FSIC/CO. Expect a ~M5–M6 launch — still inside Y1. Also sign the Category-I embalming MOA (≤50 km) before opening, and only worry about RA 9829 if the model involves selling pre-need plans as a principal.
- **FDA LTO for the bakery: 30–90 days is confirmed as the official target; realistically 6–10 weeks,** driven by the pre-opening premises inspection and application completeness. New LTOs now run 3 years for micro/small producers (AO 2024-0015, effective Jan 2025). Time the inspection to the fit-out, not to paperwork mood.
- **Net effect on the four plans:** payback dates shift by roughly **0 months (plan 8), +1–3 months (plans 5 and 9), +1–3 months (plan 6)** versus the current plans — provided titling is parallel-tracked and typhoon-season scheduling is applied. A dirt-cheap-but-dirty seller side or agricultural land classification (DAR conversion) is the scenario that would break the corrected timelines, not routine CAR processing.
